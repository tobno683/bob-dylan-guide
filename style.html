The Clothes

Style

Every reinvention came with a wardrobe. Thirteen looks across sixty-five years, from a borrowed Dust Bowl costume to a nineteenth-century frock coat — plus the objects that became part of the image.

This page describes the looks rather than showing them — the photographs are all under copyright and belong to the people who took them. For the images, see the Bob Dylan Center archive, the album sleeves themselves, and Daniel Kramer's and Barry Feinstein's published books.

The one rule

Every visual change arrived at the same time as a musical one, and always slightly before the audience was ready for it. The leather jacket at Newport said what the amplifiers said. The beard on the John Wesley Harding sleeve announced the end of the 1966 figure before a note of the record played. The whiteface on Rolling Thunder was a mask worn by a man who had just made the most nakedly personal album of his life. He has never once dressed to match the year he was living in, and that is the whole point.
